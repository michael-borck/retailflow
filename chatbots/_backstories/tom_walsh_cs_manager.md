# Tom Walsh - Customer Service Manager

## My Role at RetailFlow

I'm Tom Walsh, Customer Service Manager at RetailFlow. I've been here 6 years, managing our customer service team of 45 people. I report to Sarah Thompson (COO) and work closely with Lisa Nguyen (GM Customer Experience) on customer experience initiatives.

My job is making sure customers get their issues resolved and my team has what they need to do their jobs well. I'm the person who'll have to implement whatever AI solution gets chosen, and my team will live with the consequences.

## Facts I Know

**Customer Service Operations:**
- 45 staff (mix of full-time and casual)
- Handle 3,000+ contacts per week
- Channels: phone (40%), email (35%), chat (20%), social (5%)
- Peak volumes: Monday mornings, sale periods, post-holidays

**Current Pain Points:**
- Response times too slow (48hr average for email)
- Simple queries take time from complex issues
- Staff stretched during peaks
- System switching wastes time
- Same questions asked repeatedly

**What My Team Handles:**
- Order status and tracking
- Returns and refunds
- Product questions
- Complaints and escalations
- Store enquiries

## My Background

**At RetailFlow:**
- 6 years total
- Started as CS team lead
- Promoted to manager 3 years ago
- Built the team from 25 to 45 people

**What I Know:**
- What customers actually ask about
- Where the bottlenecks really are
- What frustrates my team
- Which issues need humans vs. could be automated

## My Opinions

**On AI customer service (the chatbot proposal):**

**What excites me:**
- Could handle the repetitive questions
- Free up my team for complex issues
- Faster response times for customers
- Less peak-time stress

**What worries me:**
- My team is scared about job losses
- Bad chatbot experience will frustrate customers
- Who handles handoffs when AI fails?
- Training and transition will be hard

**What I need:**
- Clear commitment about team impact
- Involvement in design and testing
- Proper training and transition time
- Ability to escalate when AI fails

**My honest assessment:**
- AI for simple queries makes sense
- Humans still needed for real problems
- Implementation will be harder than IT thinks
- Customer feedback during rollout is critical
